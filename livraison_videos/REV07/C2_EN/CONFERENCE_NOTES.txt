02:53 — Understanding TFM
At each point P in the region of interest, TFM coherently sums signals from transmitter i–receiver j pairs at the transmitter → P → receiver travel times. The calculation is repeated at every point for the paths and wave modes selected in reconstruction. It is not a sum of positive magnitudes without time alignment. Path families may be direct, include one or more reflections, or mode conversions, depending on the selected model; adding paths is useful only when the model is appropriate.

04:16 — Understanding voxels
A pixel is a 2D cell; a voxel is a 3D cell. The region of interest is discretized into cells carrying reconstructed response values. Dots represent centres of cells retained at the display threshold, not individual defects. U–V, U–W and V–W projections retain the maximum along the omitted direction. A finer grid does not guarantee better physical resolution. In volumetric TFM, focusing is evaluated on the 3D grid points; voxels may also carry values produced by other volume-processing methods.
